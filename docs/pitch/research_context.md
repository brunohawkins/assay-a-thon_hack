# Research context: published findings

All DOIs were resolved via Crossref and their abstracts read via Europe PMC (2026-11-08). Claims reflect only the abstracts; **[unverified]** marks anything not checkable there.

## 1. Alzheimer's disease genetics concentrate in microglial regulatory DNA

- **Nott et al. 2019, *Science*.** The authors mapped regulatory regions in the major cell types of the human brain. Variants linked to psychiatric disorders fell mainly in neuronal enhancers and promoters, while sporadic AD variants were "largely confined to microglia enhancers." Deleting one microglia-specific enhancer that carries AD-risk variants removed *BIN1* expression in microglia, but not in neurons or astrocytes. doi:10.1126/science.aay0793
- **Corces et al. 2020, *Nature Genetics*.** Single-cell chromatin accessibility and 3D chromatin interaction maps of the adult brain were used to predict dozens of functional AD and Parkinson's SNPs and to assign target genes and cell types to GWAS loci that had none. The abstract does not give a microglia-specific enrichment figure. That detail is **[unverified]** here. doi:10.1038/s41588-020-00721-x
- **Huang et al. 2017, *Nature Neuroscience*.** Heritability analysis across 220 cell types pointed to regulation of myeloid gene expression. AD heritability was enriched within the binding sites of the transcription factor PU.1 (*SPI1*). An allele that lowers *SPI1* expression was associated with later AD onset. doi:10.1038/nn.4587
- **Kosoy et al. 2022, *Nature Genetics*.** Primary microglia from 150 donors were profiled. Fine-mapping proposed regulatory mechanisms for 21 AD risk loci and narrowed 18 of them to a single gene. Transcription-factor network analysis identified SPI1 as a key regulator. doi:10.1038/s41588-022-01149-1
- **Bellenguez et al. 2022, *Nature Genetics*.** This GWAS included 111,326 cases (clinical or proxy) and 677,663 controls. It found 75 risk loci, 42 of them new. Pathway analysis "highlighted microglia implication." doi:10.1038/s41588-022-01024-z
**Bottom line:** independent approaches place much non-coding AD risk in myeloid and microglial enhancers, with PU.1 central. Amyloid and tau pathways are also implicated, so microglia are not the only cell type involved.

## 2. Microglial identity depends on the brain environment

- **Gosselin et al. 2017, *Science*.** When human microglia were moved from brain tissue into culture, microglia-specific genes were quickly and extensively switched off. Many of these genes are linked to neurodegenerative disease or carry non-coding risk variants. doi:10.1126/science.aal3222
- **Bohlen et al. 2017, *Neuron*.** Survival of microglia outside the brain depended on astrocyte-derived factors: CSF-1/IL-34, TGF-β2 and cholesterol. Mature microglia rapidly lost signature gene expression after isolation, and engrafting them back into an intact CNS reversed that loss. The authors conclude that the microglial profile "requires continuous instructive signaling from the intact CNS." doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2017.04.043
- **Lavin et al. 2014, *Cell*.** Tissue-resident macrophage enhancer landscapes are not explained by origin alone; differentiated macrophages were reprogrammed in a new tissue. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2014.11.018
- **Transplantation into mouse brain restores in vivo-like states:**
  - Hasselmann et al. 2019: cells gained an ex vivo human microglial signature and human-specific responses to amyloid-β plaques. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2019.07.002
  - Mancuso et al. 2019: engrafted cells resembled primary human microglia; about one-third of putative AD risk genes lack adequate mouse orthologs. doi:10.1038/s41593-019-0525-x
  - Svoboda et al. 2019: transplanted cells closely resembled primary human microglia. doi:10.1073/pnas.1913541116
- **Popova et al. 2021, *Cell Stem Cell*.** Microglial states differ across culture models. In brain organoids, microglia changed neural stem cell transcription and sped up synchronized network activity, so influence runs both ways. doi:10.1016/j.stem.2021.08.015

**Bottom line:** isolated microglia lose much of the gene program that carries AD risk. A brain environment largely restores it.

## 3. One molecular layer cannot stand in for another

- **Schwanhäusser et al. 2011, *Nature*.** In more than 5,000 mammalian genes, mRNA and protein levels "correlated better than previously thought," but mRNA and protein half-lives showed no correlation. Protein abundance was controlled mainly at translation. A mixed result. doi:10.1038/nature10098
- **Liu, Beyer & Aebersold 2016, *Cell*.** This review concludes that "transcript levels by themselves are not sufficient to predict protein levels in many scenarios," especially during dynamic transitions. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.03.014
- **Buccitelli & Selbach 2020, *Nat Rev Genet*.** Protein and mRNA levels typically show "reasonable correlation," yet transcriptomics and proteomics give "non-redundant readouts." doi:10.1038/s41576-020-0258-4
- **O'Neill et al. 2016, *Nat Rev Immunol*.** Metabolic pathway changes alter immune-cell function. doi:10.1038/nri.2016.70
- **Ulland et al. 2017, *Cell*.** TREM2 deficiency, a genetic AD risk factor, disrupted mTOR signaling, ATP levels and biosynthetic metabolism in microglia. Supplying energy restored microglial clustering around plaques in mice. doi:10.1016/j.cell.2017.07.023
- **Baik et al. 2019, *Cell Metabolism*.** Amyloid-β caused microglia to shift from oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis. This was followed by a chronic tolerant state with broad energy-metabolism defects and weaker cytokine and phagocytic responses. doi:10.1016/j.cmet.2019.06.005

**Bottom line:** mRNA is a partial proxy for protein, and metabolic state is a functional microglial readout linked to AD genetics. The layers are related but do not substitute for each other.

## Scale

- **Dementia burden worldwide (GBD 2019, *Lancet Public Health* 2022):** 57.4 million cases in 2019, projected to reach 152.8 million in 2050. These figures cover all-cause dementia, not AD alone. doi:10.1016/S2468-2667(21)00249-8
- **United States (Alzheimer's Association 2024):** about 6.9 million Americans aged 65+ have Alzheimer's dementia, possibly 13.8 million by 2060; 2024 dementia care payments are estimated at $360 billion. doi:10.1002/alz.13809
- **Drug development (Cummings et al. 2014):** across 413 AD trials in 2002–2012, the overall success rate was 0.4% (99.6% failure). This predates the recent anti-amyloid antibody approvals. doi:10.1186/alzrt269
- **2024 pipeline (Cummings et al. 2024):** 164 trials of 127 drugs, down from 187 and 141 in 2023. doi:10.1002/trc2.12465
